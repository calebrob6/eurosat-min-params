How we turn a 13-band image into a handful of useful numbers

The final EuroSAT classifiers are linear, but the measurements they receive are deliberately rich. This atlas shows every distinct feature idea in the core 320-feature pool and the experimental additions behind the 252-parameter result.

The big idea

Spend computation on fixed measurements, then keep the learned model tiny

A 64 by 64 patch contains 53,248 raw values. We compress it into interpretable summaries of brightness, texture, direction, material boundaries, lines, corners, local patterns, and region size. Feature selection keeps only the few summaries that add complementary information.

What counts as a parameter?

The formulas on this page are fixed programs and contain no learned values. The project counts only the weights and biases learned by logistic regression. This makes the result a study of learned state, not necessarily runtime or source-code size.

171 parameters

The 94% model selects 18 features: five spectral percentiles, three gradients, one orientation entropy, two band correlations, five index-texture features, one line feature, and one corner feature.

279 parameters

The 95% model selects 30 features: ten spectral summaries, five gradients, two orientation entropies, two band correlations, eight index-texture features, and three corner features.

252 parameters

The experimental 95% model selects 27 features: 22 from the 305-feature o6 sub-pool, two corners and one line from the rest of the core pool, plus one experimental local-binary-pattern feature and one blob feature.

Follow the information as it is compressed

Each illustration uses a synthetic patch so the relevant signal is easy to see. The real code applies the same calculation independently across thousands of EuroSAT patches and selected Sentinel-2 bands.

Spectral distributions

Every Sentinel-2 band responds to a different part of the electromagnetic spectrum. Water, healthy vegetation, dry soil, and concrete therefore produce different 13-band profiles even when their RGB colors look similar.

For each band, the core pool stores the mean, standard deviation, and the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles. The simple baseline also uses minimum and maximum. Percentiles are less sensitive to one unusual pixel and preserve more of the distribution's shape.

13 bands x (mean + stdev + 5 percentiles) = 91 features

What the animation shows: the line is the band mean, the narrow boxes are the middle percentiles, and the whiskers show a wider range. The whole profile changes as the synthetic surface moves between vegetation-like and built-like reflectance.

02Core171: 3279: 5

Multiscale gradient texture

A gradient measures how quickly pixel values change from one location to the next. Large gradients mark edges: field boundaries, roads, rooftops, shorelines, and changes in vegetation.

We calculate gradient magnitude, summarize its mean and standard deviation, average-pool the image by two, and repeat for three scales. Fine scales see small texture; coarse scales see larger structures.

sqrt(gx^2 + gy^2), then mean and stdev at 64, 32, and 16 pixels

Why it mattered: adding one gradient scale moved mean-plus-stdev from 87.8% to about 92.6%. Three scales were enough to push a linear classifier past 95%.

03Core building block

Normalized-difference index maps

Band ratios turn spectral physics into interpretable maps. NDVI contrasts near-infrared and red light to emphasize vegetation. NDWI emphasizes water, NDBI built surfaces, NDMI moisture, NBR burn-related contrast, and BSI bare soil.

The ratio removes much of the overall brightness and keeps relative spectral contrast. In the deployed feature pool, the full per-pixel maps feed later texture, line, and corner calculations rather than being reduced immediately to one patch mean.

NDVI(x, y) = (NIR(x, y) - RED(x, y)) / (NIR(x, y) + RED(x, y) + epsilon)

What the animation shows: a road has only a modest effect on the mean band values, but it becomes a sharp low-NDVI stripe inside a vegetated patch.

04CorePool: 26

Structure-tensor coherence

Gradient magnitude cannot tell a road from random rough texture if both have equally strong edges. Coherence asks whether the local gradients agree on a direction.

We average the gradient outer products into a two-by-two structure tensor. If one eigen-direction dominates, the tensor is elongated and coherence is near one. If energy is similar in every direction, the tensor is round and coherence is near zero.

coherence = sqrt((Sxx - Syy)^2 + 4 Sxy^2) / (Sxx + Syy + epsilon)

Interpretation: aligned crop rows and roads are coherent; irregular forest texture is not. We calculate one value per band at two scales.

Orientation histograms and entropy

Coherence compresses direction into one second-moment score. Orientation histograms retain the full distribution: which directions are present and how much edge strength points each way.

We use unsigned angles, so a line pointing left and the same line pointing right share a bin. Histogram counts are weighted by gradient magnitude. Shannon entropy then measures how concentrated or spread out the distribution is.

entropy = -sum(p[angle] x log(p[angle] + epsilon))

Difference from coherence: one strong edge can dominate coherence, while entropy still sees whether the remaining edge field contains one direction, two directions, or many.

06Fourier peakRadial slope

Frequency-domain texture

Repeating crop rows may be difficult to summarize directly in image space, but they create distinct peaks in the two-dimensional Fourier power spectrum.

The core feature measures the strongest mid-frequency power divided by the average power in an annulus, then applies log1p. The experimental radial-slope feature averages power by radius and fits the slope of log power against log radius.

peakiness = log(1 + max(mid-frequency power) / mean(mid-frequency power))

Two related questions: peakiness asks whether one repeating scale is unusually strong; radial slope asks whether the scene is generally dominated by coarse or fine structure.

07Core171: 2279: 2

Cross-band spatial correlation

Two patches can have the same mean red and NIR values but differ in whether red and NIR patterns occur in the same places. Pearson correlation measures that shared spatial arrangement.

We use eight physically useful pairs such as red/NIR, green/NIR, blue/NIR, SWIR1/NIR, red/SWIR1, green/red, NIR/SWIR2, and SWIR1/SWIR2.

corr(a, b) = mean((a - mean(a)) x (b - mean(b))) / (stdev(a) x stdev(b))

What it adds: every earlier feature looked at one band at a time. Correlation is the first feature that explicitly describes how two spectral channels co-vary over space.

Texture inside index maps

A patch-level NDVI value cannot distinguish a uniform crop from a road cutting through vegetation if their averages happen to match. We therefore calculate texture directly on every NDVI, NDWI, NDBI, NDMI, NBR, and BSI map.

For each map, the core family stores spatial standard deviation, mean gradient magnitude, gradient-magnitude standard deviation, and the difference between the 90th and 10th percentiles. Experimental variants repeat texture at a coarser scale and calculate coherence on index-map gradients.

6 indices x (map stdev + gradient mean + gradient stdev + p90-p10) = 24 features

Why it was strong: material-index boundaries cancel some illumination variation and expose the shapes that separate roads, crops, water, and built surfaces.

09Core171: 1252 experiment: 1

Global Hough-line structure

Local orientation features cannot tell many short parallel edges from one long straight feature. The Hough transform lets every edge pixel vote for the lines that could pass through it. Collinear pixels accumulate in the same angle-and-offset bin.

We threshold at the 85th percentile of gradient magnitude, evaluate 60 unsigned orientations, quantize line offset in two-pixel bins, and keep three summaries: peak fraction, peak length relative to the patch diagonal, and the combined strength of the best line at the top three orientations.

3 channels (pan, NDVI, NDBI) x 3 line summaries = 9 features

The selected 94% feature: the combined strength of the top three NDVI lines. It captures global collinearity that no local gradient statistic sees.

10Core171: 1279: 3252 experiment: 2

Harris corners and junctions

A line changes strongly in one direction. A corner changes strongly in two directions. Harris response uses a locally smoothed structure tensor to identify locations where both directions contain energy.

We calculate the fraction of active-gradient pixels with positive Harris response and a scale-normalized corner-strength measure. The three source channels are the panchromatic mean, NDVI, and NDBI.

R = det(J) - 0.05 x trace(J)^2

Why it helped: intersections and block grids occur in residential and industrial scenes, while crop rows can be strongly directional without forming junctions.

11Experimental252 experiment: 1

Rotation-invariant local binary patterns

Local binary patterns describe micro-texture without caring about absolute brightness. Each of eight neighbors becomes one bit: one if it is brighter than the center, zero otherwise.

We count circular bit transitions. Patterns with at most two transitions are "uniform" and are grouped by the number of one bits; all more complicated patterns share a tenth bin. The final features are histogram entropy and the fraction of uniform patterns.

8 neighbor comparisons -> 10 rotation-invariant bins -> entropy + uniform fraction

Why it is complementary: LBP recognizes fine repeated motifs and roughness patterns that can have similar gradient magnitude but different local arrangements.

12Experimental252 experiment: 1

Connected-component blob structure

After thresholding a channel above its patch median, eight-connected component labeling divides the bright half of the image into contiguous regions.

We record the largest component as a fraction of the patch, the logarithm of the component count, and mean component size. A few large fields and many small urban regions can have the same bright-pixel fraction but very different region granularity.

largest area / patch area, log(1 + component count), mean area / patch area

Why it entered the 252-parameter model: one NDVI blob-size feature supplied region-level information not already captured by gradients, lines, or corners.

Final compression

Hundreds of candidates become 18 or 30 learned inputs

Adding a feature family to the candidate pool costs no learned parameters. The cost appears only if a feature survives selection and receives classifier weights. Backward elimination repeatedly removes the feature whose absence hurts cross-validation least.

Why a broad pool can produce a tiny model

The pool is a menu, not the deployed vector. Different families compete to explain the same images. Redundant measurements disappear, while a small number of genuinely complementary measurements survive across spectral value, local texture, direction, material boundaries, and global layout.
