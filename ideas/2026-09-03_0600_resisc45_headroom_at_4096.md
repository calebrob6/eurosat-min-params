# RESISC45 at 4,096 values: what is missing, and what the head can buy

*2026-09-03. Companion to "Towards 80% under 4,096 values" in RESULTS.md.*

## The question

The 65%/70% frontier work ended with a pool whose linear ceiling is 79.4% test.
The new target, 80% under 4,096 deployed values, is above that ceiling, so the
first job was to find out what is missing rather than to fit another head.

## Three findings that change the plan

**1. The pool is hiding three points from a linear head.** A width-1,024 ReLU
head on the same 2,084 columns reads 82.6%. Two zero-parameter expansions
recover it: a hinge at each column's mean (`|z|` in effect, +1.7 points) and
ReLUs of random signed column *pairs* (+3.3 points, matching the widest MLP).
The interaction information is width-2, exactly as it was on the linear head in
the column-dictionary section. Extra knots buy nothing; wider random directions
were already known to buy nothing.

**2. New information is worth about a point, and it comes from local pattern
occurrence.** A fourth pool (random convolutional features, random texton
histograms, colour-conditioned texture, grid-cell order statistics) moves the
linear ceiling from 79.4% to 80.6%. The colour-conditioned family is the best
per-column addition since the native-resolution rebuild (66 columns, +0.9);
random convolutional features are +1.2 for 1,536 weak columns; textons are
worth nothing next to LBP.

**3. The head cannot buy nonlinear columns one at a time.** Admitting hinge or
pair-ReLU columns to the candidate list, by ranking, by an element-wise
regrow support, or wholesale, never beats the raw list at any budget up to
5,800 deployed values, and the ranking floods with them (480 of 504 used
columns). Their value is collective: the dense head spreads small weights over
thousands. That is the MOSAIKS shape from the EuroSAT negative-results table,
now seen on the column axis of a sparse head.

## Where the budget actually goes

On the 640-column quota list (linear ceiling 81.0%) the dictionary head reads
77.9% at 2,326 values and *no better* at 3,866. Holding the list fixed and
varying only the head shows why: the search overfits its support. Atom count,
worth +6 points at 256 values, is worth nothing at 3,628 (256 atoms match
16,384); a ten-fold stronger weight decay *during the search* is worth +1.4
points at 2,092 and +1.1 at 3,628, with the best validation of any arm. The
1e-4 decay has been used unchanged since iteration 4, where it was tuned at
512 values.

Frontier after this: **79.57% test at 3,628 deployed values** (16,384-atom
class dictionary on the quota list, search decay 1e-3). 0.4 points short.

## Lessons

* A single per-column ranking over a widened pool is the wrong candidate list
  every time a redundant family is added -- iteration 3's quota is a general
  rule, not a one-off fix. Build the list from separate rankings and check its
  *own* linear ceiling before fitting anything on it.
* Regime changes are the norm, not the exception, when the budget moves 4x:
  the power-of-two `sigma`, the atom count and the search decay all flipped
  sign between 512 and 3,584 values. Every setting tuned in the starved regime
  needs re-checking in the new one.
* Print train accuracy next to test in every support-search experiment. The
  plateau was invisible in the validation-selected numbers and obvious in the
  0.87 -> 0.92 train climb against a 0.78 test flat line.

## What this suggests next

* **Regularise the search, not the refit.** Sweep the search's weight decay
  (3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3), its epoch count and its stop fraction at 2,048-3,584
  values on the quota list; the refit's `C` is already validation-selected and
  cannot fix a bad support.
* **Learn a few hidden units instead of buying random ones.** A width-64 MLP on
  the 512 list reads 80.8% and width-128 reads 82.4%; a sparse first layer
  (RigL on `W1`) with a coded second layer is the head that would read the
  pair-ReLU information without paying per random pair.
* **Widen the raw quota list, not the expansion.** 448/128/64 and 384/128/128
  differ by 0.3 points; the fourth pool's 128-column quota was a guess and the
  colour-conditioned family is only 66 columns wide.
* **Price the knots honestly if hinges ever pay.** A hinge at the column mean
  costs one deployed value per column; a power-of-two-rounded knot would cost
  bits instead, the same trick that made the pair dictionary's `sigma` cheap.
