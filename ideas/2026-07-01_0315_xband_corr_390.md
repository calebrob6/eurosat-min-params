- **xcorr is the winner**: highest CV at k=38 (0.9416, 10-seed; 0.9414, 20-seed),
  best test (0.9467), and it improves the model at *every* k (k=40 test 0.9485 —
  the best test seen on any pool). Cross-band correlation is genuinely new signal:
  every prior feature is per-band, so band *co-variation* was completely unseen.
- **variogram and homogeneity HURT.** They reach CV lower than base at the
  selected k — the ~50 extra features are mostly redundant with the existing
  multi-scale gradient stats and just add L1-ranking noise. Coarseness is already
  captured by the multi-scale gradient-magnitude family.
- **Combining orthogonal families did NOT stack this time.** vgram+xcorr,
  xcorr+oent2, vgram+xcorr+oent2 all did *worse* than xcorr alone at the floor
  (e.g. vgram+xcorr fell back to k=40). Unlike iteration 4 (where orientation
  families stacked cleanly), adding weak/noisy families to the pool dilutes the L1
  top-k. Lesson: only add a family if it helps *alone*; don't pool speculatively.

## Honest caveat on submission 05
At the CV-selected k=38 the **val** accuracy (0.9372) is *below* 0.940, unlike
submissions 03–04 where val also cleared the bar. Both the train-CV selector
(0.9417) and test (0.9461) clear it, and the honest rule never touches val/test,
so the submission is valid. But it's a thinner margin than 410: selecting the
winning pool among several candidates lifts train-CV a hair more than it lifts
this val split (the classic multiple-comparison symptom). If a wider cushion is
wanted, xcorr at k=40 gives 410 params / test **0.9480** (best test on the
frontier) with val 0.9378.

## Why xcorr works (physical intuition)
NDVI-style vegetation reflects strongly in NIR and weakly in RED, and does so
*coherently across the patch* — so RED and NIR spatial patterns are strongly
(anti-)correlated for crops/forest but much less so for built-up/water where the
two bands respond to different materials. Coherence/orientation see one band's
edges; xcorr sees whether two bands' whole spatial patterns move together. That's
the orthogonal bit.

## Ranked ideas for next iterations
1. **Target Highway (0.857) & PermanentCrop (0.901) directly — they still cap
   accuracy** and are unchanged since submission 03. Every recent gain was on the
   average, not these two. Ideas: (a) a feature that separates linear paved roads
   (Highway) from field boundaries — e.g. the *longest* connected high-coherence
   run, or coherence *at the patch scale* (single dominant direction across the
   whole 64×64, not local); (b) crop-row periodicity strength restricted to the
   NIR/RED bands where PermanentCrop rows show up.
2. **Spatial texture of spectral INDICES, not bands.** Current index features use
   per-band *mean* ratios (one scalar NDVI/patch), discarding all spatial info.
   Compute the per-pixel NDVI/NDWI *map* and take its std / gradient / entropy —
   within-patch vegetation heterogeneity is a fresh axis (crop uniformity vs mixed
   herbaceous). Likely the strongest untried lever; genuinely orthogonal.
3. **Prune the 65 percentile features to de-noise the L1 top-k.** Many of the 273
   pool features are redundant percentiles; a cleaner, smaller pool could let the
   L1 top-38 be a better subset and possibly qualify k=37 (380) or k=36 (370).
4. **k=35 (360) needs ~0.006 more CV** — beyond one orthogonal family. Probably
   requires idea 2 (index-texture) *plus* pruning, or a smarter-than-L1 subset
   selector (forward-selection on CV, watched for overfitting).
5. **Still haven't used the 8 idle V100s.** For a >95% sub-500-param target, a
   tiny distilled conv net remains the one unexplored direction.
